New Publication 📄 <br>
<br>
How well do political parties adapt their issue strategies to different social media platforms? And do these strategies actually generate engagement? <br>
<br>
In our new paper in [Political Communication](https://doi.org/10.1080/10584609.2026.2715702), Simon Lübke, Anna-Katharina Wurst, Jörg Haßler, and I examine party communication on Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok during the 2025 German federal election campaign. Combining a manual quantitative content analysis with survey data from the German Longitudinal Election Study (GLES), we investigate three established issue strategies: issue ownership, riding the wave, and issue yield. Our results show:<br>
 <br>
- Parties frequently prioritize issues they “own” and issues that are salient on the general public agenda.<br>
- But these strategies do not always match what audiences on individual platforms value.<br>
- Engagement is highest when parties address platform-specific issue preferences, particularly when an issue resonates both with a party’s supporters and with the wider user base on the platform.<br>

👉 In other words: party communication is primarily aligned with the preferences of the general public, but effective digital campaigning requires understanding what different platform audiences actually care about.<br>
<br>
The findings shed new light on how parties compete over issues in an increasingly platformized campaign environment and on the challenges of translating established campaign strategies into the diverse worlds of Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok.<br>
